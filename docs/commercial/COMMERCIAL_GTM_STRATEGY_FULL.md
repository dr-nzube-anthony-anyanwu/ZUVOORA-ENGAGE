# 1. Executive summary

ZUVOORA Engage should enter the Nigerian market as a focused, eye-care-specific patient engagement product. Its first job is to make an existing clinic website more responsive: answer approved administrative questions, capture enquiries and support appointment booking when connected to a scheduling provider. It is the entry point to the broader ZUVOORA ecosystem, not a substitute for the full operating system.

| Commercial thesis / Sell a measurable operational outcome—faster enquiry handling, better patient access and easier booking—not the novelty of an AI chatbot. |

| --- |



# 2. Market problem and customer hypothesis

The initial market hypothesis is that some Nigerian eye-care businesses have static websites and rely heavily on phone or messaging channels. Visitors may not find answers outside opening hours, and staff may repeatedly answer the same questions. A website assistant with clinic-specific knowledge and optional booking could improve access and reduce repetitive administrative work.

This hypothesis must be validated clinic by clinic. Website traffic, current enquiry volume, booking processes, staff workload and willingness to pay will vary. A chatbot is unlikely to create much value for a clinic with almost no website traffic or digital enquiries unless paired with a separate digital visibility initiative.

# 5. Value proposition and messaging

## 5.1 Primary promise

Make your eye clinic website responsive around the clock, help visitors find trusted clinic information and make it easier to request or book an appointment.

## 5.2 Supporting value propositions

- After-hours information access: visitors can ask administrative questions when staff are unavailable.

- Reduced repetitive enquiries: staff can spend less time answering routine questions.

- Booking accessibility: visitors can check availability and book through a connected scheduler.

- Operational visibility: owners can see common questions, handovers and booking activity.

- Low-disruption adoption: add the widget to an existing website rather than replacing it.

- Eye-care relevance: clinic service templates and terminology designed with optometry/ophthalmology domain knowledge.

# 5.3 Claims to avoid

- Do not promise a guaranteed increase in patient numbers, revenue or conversion.

- Do not claim the assistant diagnoses, triages or replaces clinicians.

- Do not claim 100% accuracy or zero hallucinations.

- Do not claim legal or security certification unless independently obtained.

- Do not imply every clinic needs AI or that every website visitor will book.

# 6. Competitive landscape and positioning

The market includes broad clinic-management platforms, healthcare communications products, AI receptionist vendors and international patient-engagement systems. Examples identified for ongoing competitor monitoring include ClinicOS, Clinimu, Support.ng, Maraba AI, MediHost AI and MediBY in Nigeria, plus international products such as Lummy AI, Weave and Smith.ai. Product scope, availability and public claims can change; verify current offerings before using competitor comparisons in sales material.

| Competitive category | Typical strengths | ZUVOORA Engage response |

| --- | --- | --- |

| Clinic management / EMR platforms | Patient records, billing, scheduling, clinic workflows | Do not compete head-on in the first release; focus on website engagement and connect to existing systems. |

| Generic AI chatbot builders | Flexible, broad use cases, low setup barriers | Offer eye-care-specific onboarding, clinic knowledge governance, booking and support. |

| AI receptionist / voice platforms | Phone automation, appointment workflows | Start with website-first, lower-complexity adoption; add voice after validation. |

| Patient communication platforms | Reminders, messaging, multi-channel tools | Differentiate through eye-care configuration and simple website integration. |

| International clinic SaaS | Mature workflows and integrations | Localise pricing, onboarding, support and workflow assumptions for Nigerian eye-care practices. |



# 7. Three-layer product and commercial packaging

| Layer | Target buyer | Commercial approach |

| --- | --- | --- |

| Layer 1 — Engage Essentials | Smaller clinic wanting website chatbot and enquiry support | Lower setup fee + monthly subscription; bounded usage allowance. |

| Layer 2 — Engage Advanced | Clinic wanting scheduling, reminders, WhatsApp/voice or branches | Higher subscription or add-ons; provider accounts and variable messaging/voice charges clearly separated. |

| Layer 3 — Full ZUVOORA | Clinic/hospital seeking broader eye-care operating system | Modular platform proposal covering Core and relevant modules; Engage is integrated, not a disconnected duplicate. |



Feature entitlements should be managed centrally. A clinic should be able to upgrade without a new application or data migration to an unrelated system. Package boundaries, included usage, overage rates, support scope and third-party costs must be explicit.

The following are introductory test ranges, not validated market prices. They should be adjusted after discovery interviews, pilot costs and willingness-to-pay evidence.

| Package | Illustrative setup | Illustrative monthly | Included scope |

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Engage Essentials | ₦150,000–₦200,000 | ₦15,000–₦25,000 | Website widget, clinic knowledge base, FAQs, enquiry inbox, basic analytics and email enquiry alerts. |

| Engage Professional | ₦250,000–₦350,000 | ₦35,000–₦50,000 | Essentials plus one scheduling provider, booking, confirmation, reminder workflow and enhanced reporting. |

| Engage Premium | ₦450,000–₦750,000+ | ₦65,000–₦100,000+ | Professional plus selected WhatsApp/voice, multi-branch and advanced workflow capabilities. |



## 8.1 Pricing principles

- Charge setup for onboarding, content preparation, domain/widget integration, provider connection and testing.

- Monthly subscription pays for platform access, defined support, monitoring and an included usage allowance.

- State clearly whether AI inference, email, WhatsApp, SMS, voice and scheduling provider fees are included, passed through or customer-owned.

- Use usage caps and transparent overage rules; do not promise unlimited AI usage at a low fixed price.

- Offer client-owned API/provider accounts where the clinic wants direct control of provider billing.

- Do not absorb unlimited website edits, custom workflows or support in the base subscription.

- Charge separately for bespoke enterprise integrations after discovery and written scope.

# 8.2 Example unit economics model

For every tenant, track monthly revenue less attributable costs: AI tokens, hosting allocation, email, messaging, scheduling-related fees where applicable, support time, payment processing and customer success. Review gross margin by package and cohort. Do not set final package prices until real pilot usage is measured.

| Cost item | Measurement approach |

| --- | --- |

| AI inference | Provider/model, input/output tokens, estimated and invoiced cost per tenant. |

| Hosting | Allocate infrastructure cost using usage or a defensible shared-cost method. |

| Email | Messages, bounces and provider invoice by tenant where available. |

| Support | Onboarding hours and monthly support time per customer. |

| Messaging/voice | Pass-through or separately priced usage based on provider billing. |

| Payment fees/tax | Include applicable payment processing and tax treatment after professional advice. |



# 9. Customer discovery plan

Before broad development or pricing lock-in, interview at least 10–15 target clinics, including the doctors who previously expressed interest. The goal is not to pitch immediately; it is to understand current workflows and quantify the problem.

- How many website visits or enquiries do you receive in a typical month?

- What are the five most common questions patients ask before visiting?

- What happens to enquiries after clinic closing time?

- How do patients currently book appointments?

- How many calls or messages are missed or delayed in a typical week?

- Who updates clinic opening hours, services and prices online?

- What happens when an appointment is cancelled or rescheduled?

- Would an automated assistant be acceptable to your patients and staff?

- What would make you trust or distrust it?

- What measurable result would justify a monthly subscription?

- What budget and procurement process would apply?

- Would you prefer to own your scheduling/email provider account or have ZubeVision manage it?

# 10. Pilot strategy — first three clinics

| Pilot | Profile | Validation objective |

| --- | --- | --- |

| Pilot A | Small independent optometry practice | Validate setup speed, FAQ accuracy, widget adoption and enquiry value. |

| Pilot B | Medium eye clinic | Validate scheduling adapter, booking confirmation, reminders and staff workflow. |

| Pilot C | Larger or multi-service eye hospital | Validate higher usage, roles, branches, escalation and reporting. |



## 10.1 Pilot terms

- Use a written pilot agreement defining scope, duration, responsibilities, data handling, support and exit process.

- Use a defined pilot period, for example 6–8 weeks, after the clinic has completed onboarding and acceptance testing.

- Agree baseline metrics before activation.

- Do not use real patient health data in demonstrations or testing unless the clinic has approved the lawful purpose and safeguards.

- Define a discounted founding-customer price in exchange for structured feedback, not an indefinite free service.

- Obtain permission before using the clinic name, logo, testimonials or performance results in marketing.

## 10.2 Pilot success criteria

| Dimension | Evidence of success |

| --- | --- |

| Adoption | Real visitors use the widget; clinic staff review conversations. |

| Accuracy | Sampled answers match approved clinic information; unsupported-answer rate is low. |

| Operational usefulness | Staff report reduced repetitive responses or better visibility of enquiries. |

| Booking reliability | Confirmed bookings match provider records; no duplicate or false confirmations. |

| Notification reliability | Reminders are timely, consent-aware and suppressed after cancellation/rescheduling. |

| Economics | Cost per conversation and support burden fit the proposed package. |

| Willingness to pay | Clinic renews at an agreed price or provides a credible purchase commitment. |



# 11. Go-to-market plan

## 11.1 Initial geography

Start in Abuja because the founder has local professional context and can conduct face-to-face discovery, demos and onboarding. Expand to Lagos and other Nigerian cities only after repeatable onboarding and support are proven.

## 11.2 Sales motion

1. Build a polished demo clinic website and a live Engage tenant using synthetic, clearly labelled sample information.

1. Create a shortlist of 20–30 eye-care businesses with existing websites and visible digital enquiry channels.

1. Prioritise warm introductions and clinics that previously expressed interest.

1. Conduct a short discovery conversation and inspect the clinic's current website and enquiry process.

1. Demonstrate the assistant using approved/public clinic information or a permission-based clinic-specific demo.

1. Present a one-page proposal with the exact package, setup scope, monthly fee, included usage, third-party costs and exclusions.

1. Complete onboarding, content approval, domain verification and acceptance tests.

1. Review results after 2, 4 and 6–8 weeks; seek renewal and permission-based testimonial.

## 11.3 Demonstration script

1. Show the clinic website on a mobile phone.

1. Ask a common administrative question such as opening hours or whether a service is offered.

1. Ask a second question that tests whether the assistant admits missing information instead of guessing.

1. Start an appointment journey and show actual availability from the connected provider.

1. Complete a test booking in a sandbox or agreed test environment.

1. Show confirmation and clinic notification.

1. Show the clinic dashboard and how staff can take over an enquiry.

1. Explain that the clinic can start with Essentials and add modules later.

# 12. Sales qualification and objection handling

| Objection | Recommended response |

| --- | --- |

| We already have WhatsApp. | Engage does not replace WhatsApp. It helps visitors get answers on the website and can route unresolved enquiries to your existing channels. |

| Our receptionist already answers questions. | The goal is not to replace staff. It handles routine questions and after-hours enquiries so staff can focus on people who need human assistance. |

| Our website gets little traffic. | Then we should first assess whether a chatbot is the right investment. We should not sell a product that cannot show value; digital visibility may need separate attention. |

| AI may give wrong medical advice. | Engage is designed for administrative information, not diagnosis. It uses clinic-approved content, has safe boundaries and escalates clinical questions. |

| Why a monthly fee? | The subscription covers ongoing hosting, monitoring, AI service, updates, support and agreed usage—not a one-time code delivery. |

| We do not want another provider account. | We can assess a managed-provider arrangement or customer-owned account, with costs and responsibilities stated clearly. |

| Can you build a special feature? | We assess whether it belongs in the shared roadmap. Unique enterprise work is separately scoped and priced. |



# 13. Customer onboarding and retention

- Provide a simple onboarding checklist and named clinic contact.

- Help the clinic verify domain ownership and install the widget.

- Require clinic approval of services, hours, fees, policies and FAQ content.

- Train owner/admin/receptionist on inbox, handover and escalation.

- Review conversation samples and knowledge gaps during the first month.

- Send a concise monthly usage/performance report.

- Review plan fit and usage before renewal; avoid surprise overage bills.

- Provide a clear support channel, response expectations and service status communication.

# 14. Partnerships and channels

- Professional optometry and ophthalmology networks, subject to appropriate permissions and ethical marketing.

- Website design/development agencies serving clinics.

- Clinic IT consultants and practice-management vendors.

- Relevant eye-care conferences, continuing professional development events and professional communities.

- Referral programme for existing clinics, with transparent terms.

# 15. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |

| --- | --- |

| Competitors offer broader bundles | Own the eye-care website engagement niche; integrate rather than rebuild EMR. |

| Price resistance | Offer Essentials entry tier; demonstrate value; qualify traffic and enquiry volume. |

| Long sales cycle | Start with owner-led independent practices and warm referrals. |

| Feature creep | Roadmap governance; build reusable modules only when evidence supports them. |

| Churn after novelty fades | Monthly reporting, staff workflow integration, quality reviews and reliable support. |

| AI cost rises | Meter usage, model routing, quotas, pricing review and provider abstraction. |

| Clinic does not maintain content | Review reminders, content owner and stale-content warnings. |

| Security/privacy concern | Transparent controls, contracts, minimal data, documented incident process and independent review as business grows. |

# 18. Founder operating principles

- Do not build a feature solely because it sounds impressive; validate a buyer problem.

- Do not promise a feature until provider access, security and operational support are verified.

- Prefer standard packages and configuration over bespoke forks.

- Price third-party variable costs transparently.

- Use customer feedback and measured outcomes to guide the roadmap.

- Protect the founder's engineering time with clear scope, support boundaries and change control.

- Use Engage as an entry product while preserving the full ZUVOORA ecosystem vision.
